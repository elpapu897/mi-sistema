# Social Content OS — Framework Reference

The shared strategy layer for the whole pack. Every skill leans on this. Distilled
from the operating playbook: Interest Graph, Graffiti execution, ABC, SGTMS,
the Awareness Funnel, Best of Nine, JSON Prompting, and ManyChat monetization.

---

## 1. The shift: Follower Graph → Interest Graph

Reach is no longer a function of how many people follow you. The algorithm
shows every post to a small test audience first; if they engage (watch time,
saves, shares, completion), distribution scales. If they don't, the post dies —
regardless of follower count.

Consequence: **a zero-follower account can beat a legacy brand** when the content
triggers the right interest signals. You are not entertaining existing fans. You
are stopping a stranger ("a friend in waiting") mid-scroll.

| | Gallery Era (dead) | Street Market Era (now) |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | Follower count | Per-post performance |
| Production | Polished, curated, slow | Raw, authentic, high-velocity |
| Goal | Keep fans engaged | Stop a stranger mid-scroll |
| Cadence | Quality over quantity | Volume over perfection |

## 2. The Graffiti mindset

Hit the wall, get the message up, move to the next target. While a competitor
storyboards one masterpiece, you ship 10 variations and let the algorithm tell
you which one won.

- **Speed and volume are the moat.** Test many, keep winners, kill the rest.
- **First ~150 posts = finding your voice**, not chasing ROI (Hormozi). Decade
  thinking beats 90-day sprints.
- **Repeatability over originality.** Reinventing the wheel every post stalls
  growth. Identify formats that work and iterate them like a late-night show
  with recurring segments.

## 3. ABC — anatomy of any single piece

Every post must do all three. Drop one and the post is dead.

- **A — Attention (first 3s):** break thumb muscle-memory. See the hook taxonomy.
- **B — Benefit:** deliver the promised value. Reader must feel their time paid
  off in knowledge, entertainment, or inspiration. Plain language, no fluff.
- **C — Close:** one clear, low-friction CTA. Lower the barrier: not "buy my
  course" but "comment WORD and I'll DM you the checklist" (ManyChat).

## 4. Hook taxonomy

The first frame decides everything. A strong hook makes the brain switch from
autopilot to attention — it should be strong enough that a user scrolls back up.

- **Curiosity Gap** — open a loop the brain needs to close. ("This coffee should
  not taste good...")
- **Pattern Interrupt** — visual or conceptual contrast. ("I eat carbs every day
  and I'm lean at 45.")
- **Challenge a Common Belief** — the strongest trigger. Negate industry dogma.
  ("Picking a niche is making you invisible.")
- **Negation** — "Why you should NOT do X."
- **Number / concrete result** — "How I got [result] in [time]."
- **Before / After** — visual or narrative transformation.

The Triple-Threat hook synchronizes three layers in the first second:
**Visual** (decluttered frame, single focal point) + **Written** (clear over
clever, bold promise or sharp pain) + **Audio** (voiceover/sonic cue setting
context). Clutter creates instant rejection — keep frame one clean.

Weak vs strong:
- "How to make a vanilla latte" → "This coffee should not taste this good."
- "My marketing tips for 2024" → "Most founders have zero clarity on this."
- "A list of my favorite tools" → "In 2022 I did X, in 2023 I did Y, then this happened."

## 5. SGTMS — the operating loop

The assembly line that removes guessing.

- **S — Start:** one strong idea/insight rooted in a customer problem.
- **G — Generate:** 10 variations (hooks, formats) of that one idea.
- **T — Test:** publish in a tight window to get clean data.
- **M — Measure:** hard metrics — retention/watch-time, completion rate, saves,
  CTR, cost-per-lead. Not likes.
- **S — Scale:** identify winners and repurpose across focused channels.

## 6. Best of Nine

How to find a repeatable format instead of guessing. Pick **3 proven formats**,
recreate each **3 times** (changing only one variable: hook, background, audio).
Then look at what the winners share (audio? color? hook structure?) and double
down. Separates a lucky algorithm spike from a real repeatable format.

## 7. Awareness Funnel (content per stage)

Match content to where the viewer is. Most brands jump straight to the sale —
real money is in the nurture middle.

1. **Unaware** — show a problem they don't know they have. Visual metaphors
   (e.g. a stack of sugar cubes next to a "healthy" drink).
2. **Problem-aware** — present solutions, build authority, tell your story.
3. **Consideration** — prove your method is best: behind-the-scenes, unique
   method/ingredients, social proof.
4. **Decision** — remove objections: testimonials, FAQ, the offer.
5. **Conversion** — direct offer, time-bound, fast-action bonus.

## 8. The monetization layer (funnel)

"Likes don't feed your kids." Reach without revenue is a vanity metric.

- **ToF (Discovery):** short forms (Reels/Shorts) building curiosity + reach.
- **MoF (Nurture):** longer content (YouTube, newsletter, case studies). This
  is where trust is built. Skipping it = "asking for marriage on the first date."
- **BoF (Conversion):** direct CTA + offers for buy-ready people.

**ManyChat = the Automated Handshake.** When a post spikes, attention fades in
minutes. ManyChat works 24/7, turning comments/DMs into leads (lead magnets,
calendar bookings) before the buzz dies. The comment-trigger CTA ("comment WORD")
is the bridge from Discovery to Nurture.

## 9. Niche of One + Topic Tree

Don't be a "search engine with a face" — AI replaces that. Be a unique synthesis.

- **Niche of One** — you as the foundation: philosophy + unique perspective.
  Your only insurance against being replaced by AI.
- **3 broad interests** — wide markets (e.g. Business, Health, Spirituality).
  Diversification makes the brand stable like a portfolio.
- **Subtopics** — concrete areas (AI marketing, strength training, meditation).

Broad topics grow (top of funnel); narrow topics convert (newsletter, offers).
Synthesis across fields builds an authority AI can't copy.

## 10. Carousel mechanics

Ignoring carousels costs ~30% reach. They give the algorithm multiple entry
points (the post can resurface from different slides) and high dwell time.

- **Format 1080×1350** (4:5). Maximum vertical screen real estate, kills
  distractions, forces focus.
- **7–10 slides** = peak engagement and dwell time.
- **Slide 1 — Scroll-Stop:** high-energy visual + a hook on a specific pain point.
- **Middle — Tension & Value:** alternate light/dark backgrounds ("rhythm check")
  to sustain attention; match visual energy to narrative energy (high-contrast on
  hook/tension slides, calmer on teaching slides).
- **Final — Identity CTA:** align the CTA with who the viewer wants to become
  ("build your identity stack"), plus the comment-trigger.
- **Add music** so the carousel also enters the Reels feed = free non-follower reach.

## 11. Caption types

People scan, they don't read. Short sentences, no jargon, scannable formatting.

1. **Relatable story** — your failure that became a lesson.
2. **Tool list** — "5 tools that save me 10h/week."
3. **Surprising fact** — "Why 90% of marketing strategies fail in month one."
4. **Before / After** — the transformation.
5. **Simple question** — "A or B? Tell me in the comments."
6. **Educational body copy** — expand the graphic's idea in short, plain sentences.

LinkedIn / text-post skeletons: **PAS** (Problem → Agitate → Solution) or **BAB**
(Before → After → Bridge). One idea per post. First two lines must clarify the
topic and trigger a personal concern. Add a visual anchor (infographic/chart).

## 12. JSON Prompting (brand-consistent visuals without reference images)

Feed the image layer a JSON spec of the brand (hex palette, typography, mood,
composition) so every asset stays architecturally on-brand across sessions.
Use **asset batching** — generate 30–50 assets in one session to kill production
downtime. Use AI for **reverse engineering**: analyze the pacing/hooks of market
leaders and adapt the structure (not the content) into your own process.

## 13. Multi-channel / Content Flywheel

Mixing formats on one account confuses the algorithm and blurs the signal. Split
channels for signal clarity, scalability, and lower-stakes testing.

- **Long-form** is the deep foundation (newsletter / YouTube).
- **Deconstruct** it into carousels + text posts (that's the `repurpose` skill).
- **Generate** B-roll/asset packs for the short forms.
- **Distribute** to LinkedIn, X, Instagram, TikTok — each adapted to channel norms.

## Quality bar for everything this pack outputs

- Hook in the first line/frame, always. No warm-up.
- Clear over clever. Plain language. Short sentences. Scannable.
- One idea per asset. One CTA, low friction.
- Specific beats generic — real numbers, real stories, a real point of view.
- Map every asset to a funnel stage; never ship a post with no next step.
- No AI tells in human-facing copy: no em-dashes, no en-dashes, no smart quotes,
  no "in today's fast-paced world", no "unlock/unleash/elevate/dive in/game-changer".
